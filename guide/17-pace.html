A pace that works

The time from here to the deadline as I would pace it: what to do in what order and why, the lectures that arrive while you work, how many hours each part takes, and why final's runs come first and should be finished by about December 1.

About 16 minutes to read.

How to use this page

There are no checkpoints in Project 3, and nothing is checked on the dates below. Everything is due once, on Wednesday, December 9, at 11:59 pm, and that date is firm: nothing is accepted after it. This page is the pace I would keep, so you can plan the time that is left around your other courses. The dates are waypoints, the pace I would keep, and not deadlines.

This page answers "when"; uv run p3 next answers "what now". On any day, it says where you are on the project's map and the one next thing to do, with the guide page that explains it, and the Guide runs it for you whenever you ask. The map on Start here has the same twelve stages.

The whole plan rests on one fact: a bench run goes on by itself in a plain terminal, a terminal with no Claude Code in it, while you do something else. In my pilot one full run took about 6 minutes for the baseline and about 17 for a config with review loops, plus its scoring, and students' final runs have taken 11 to 27 minutes each; almost none of that is your time. So start long runs as early as you can, and do your reading and writing while they run.

Until Tuesday, November 24: setup, the baseline, steps 1 and 2

On the map, that is stages 1 to 5, and stage 6 for the length study.

  • On the first day: set up your repo (Set up your repo), open the Guide and the Builder (The Guide and the Builder), make the quick run of c0-baseline, and start its three full runs (Run the baseline). Setup is the step with the most ways to go wrong, and I would like it behind you while there is plenty of time.
  • While the baseline runs: read its quick run's outputs, KNOWN-GAPS.md and reviews/README.md, and write your PREFERENCES.md (Write your PREFERENCES.md). None of that needs a run, and the full runs use the snapshot in configs/c0-baseline/, so you can work in tutor/ while they go.
  • Then step 1 (Step 1: four skills): build the teach, tutor, quiz and grade skills in quick rounds, usually four rounds or more; snapshot c1-skills; make its full run; write its major review; then its prompt in DECISIONS.md and its entries in reviews/components.md.
  • Step 2 (Step 2: review loops): reviewer subagents on teach, quiz and grade, added one per quick round, so at least three rounds, with checklists drawn from your c1-skills review; then the snapshot, the full run and the major review.
  • 598E, and 498E with the length-study stretch (Stretch options and the 598E length study): commit PREREG.md, then make the study's four teach-only runs of c1-skills, since they need only c1-skills. PREREG.md has to be committed before the first of the study's eight teach-only runs, because git history is what proves the order.

The c1-skills major review is what step 2 is built from: the problems it names, with their item ids and quotes, become the checklists of your reviewers. If you are in 498E and taking a stretch other than the length study, pick it early and try a small piece of it first, because most options draw on your Claude plan.

Until Sunday, November 29: step 3 and your test topic's items

Thursday, November 26, is Thanksgiving, with no class, so this stretch is shaped by a long weekend. On the map, that is stages 7 and 8.

  • Step 3 (Step 3: split the teach job): the teach job split across subagents of your design, in quick rounds, then the snapshot, the full run and the major review, which covers the lesson only.
  • Your test topic (Your test topic): write the items and commit them. They take about 1.75 hours, they need no run, and they must be committed before their first run, which comes once final is frozen. That makes them good work for the time a step's full run takes.

Lecture 25 (Tuesday, November 24) is an open discussion. If you can, finish step 3's review before the weekend ends, because final keeps what earned its place, and you only know that once all three steps are measured.

final and its runs, by about Tuesday, December 1

On the map, that is stage 9, up to the point where the runs are finished.

  1. Freeze final with the guard, as early as you can (final: the guard). final is what earned its place, plus a guard hook that keeps the answer keys from the tutor's main conversation, with its tests, and usually a grader subagent with a quick round of its own. Snapshot it, and commit tutor/.

  2. Start the runs that need it (final: the runs). uv run p3 next lists its two repeats, then the ablation, a final-minus-<x> snapshot (final with one component removed) and its one run, which may be made with --only and the scenarios the removed component touches, then the runs of c0-baseline and final on your test topic. Every one of them runs a frozen snapshot, so their order does not matter, as long as you bring tutor/ back with git restore tutor/ after the ablation's snapshot and before your learning session. For the length study, also the four teach-only runs of final; try one 60-minute run first, because a teach scenario has 45 minutes at every length.

  3. Read and write while they run, as the next section describes, and if a usage limit stops a run, finish it with uv run p3 bench --resume after the reset.

The last week, to Wednesday, December 9

On the map, that is stages 10 to 12, and what remains of stage 9.

  • Your learning session with final and its major review.
  • The blind comparison against c0-baseline, once final's two runs are scored and its major review is committed.
  • The open review of your test topic, once its runs are in; the repeats, read the way lecture 23 (Tuesday, November 17) reads them; and the judge check, after lecture 24 (Thursday, November 19).
  • The component inventory, DECISIONS.md with its numbers from uv run p3 report --write, and the video.
Watch out

Hold the learning session on final as you snapshotted it. The ablation means taking one component out of tutor/ to snapshot it; git restore tutor/ brings final back, and the session should be with final.

Write and commit the major review of final before you start the blind comparison, so your open judgment is on record before your blind one.

Because P4's presentations fall on Thursday, December 3, and Tuesday, December 8, the last week is a busy one for everyone, and the writing is the part that fits around them: it needs no tokens. Before you post on Canvas, run uv run p3 check --final and push your last commit (Hand in).

Why this order

  • Long runs first. A long run takes from a few minutes to most of an hour on the clock, and only a few minutes of your attention. A run started early leaves room for what can go wrong: a usage limit, a scenario that fails, a run you have to repeat. A run started on the last day leaves none.
  • Reading and writing while runs go. Most of your hours go to reading outputs, trying the tutor and writing about it, and much of that needs no new run. The blind comparison, p3 report, p3 check and p3 snapshot cost no tokens at all; the comparison's pairs come from runs you already have.
  • Each step is built on the one before. Your c2-review reviewers' checklists come from your c1-skills review; c3-split is measured against c2-review; final keeps what earned its place, which you only know once all three steps are measured.
  • The test topic's items before final is frozen. A test topic is committed before any run on it, so its items are on record before any result and nobody, you included, tunes the tutor to it. Its runs wait for final, so writing the items earlier means they are ready the moment final is.
  • The runs the most work depends on come first. The blind comparison needs two scored runs of final, and its major review reviews one of them; the test topic's open review needs its runs; the ablation is needed for one prompt of DECISIONS.md.
  • Finished by about December 1. P4 draws on the same Claude plan, and its presentations are Thursday, December 3, and Tuesday, December 8, so tokens that P3's runs spend in the last week would come out of the plan P4 needs. Finishing the runs by about December 1 leaves the last week for writing, which costs nothing.

Why version 0.2 asks for less

The estimates of version 0.1 ran low. The page gave 32 hours for 498E, and students reported 40 to 50, so I was wrong by a wide margin. The writing was where the time went: the reviews, EVAL.md and the inventory took far longer than the 10 hours I had planned for them. That is why version 0.2 cuts the writing: one learning session, one test topic, a short DECISIONS.md in place of EVAL.md, and fewer required parts in each review and in each inventory entry, and fewer runs to go with them.

For 598E the architecture is larger: at least five skills and six subagents, with one new component per round, so about 11 quick rounds instead of 9 and a longer inventory.

The token load of the last stretch

Every bench scenario draws on your own Claude plan, and on a subscription the cost is your usage limit, not dollars. The runs of final are when you are most likely to reach that limit.

Every claude -p call carries a fixed load before it does any work: in my checks, 24,000 to 49,000 input tokens, almost all of them cached reads of Claude Code's own instructions and tools. Every subagent pays a share of that load again, plus whatever it reads, so a tutor with many subagents uses up a weekly limit much faster than one without. In my pilot a full run of the baseline was 1.85 million tokens, and a full run of my review-loop config (four skills, with reviewers on teach, quiz and grade, at most two passes each) was 6.99 million. Students' final runs measured so far took about 3 to 7 million tokens and 11 to 27 minutes each, and two runs of 7 million tokens fill one 5-hour usage window on a Pro plan. With a final of about 7 million tokens, its two runs, the ablation on its scenarios, and the test topic's two runs come to roughly 27 million tokens, where version 0.1 needed about 45 million. --jobs 4 runs a repeat's scenarios side by side: the same tokens in about a third of the time. That is why the pace starts with final's runs: almost every token the project spends in this stretch goes to them, everything else depends on them, and finishing them by about December 1 leaves you time if a usage limit arrives late, and leaves the plan to P4.

598E, plan for the cost

Your architecture may be larger than my review-loop config, at least six subagents where mine had six, so measure it: one run of final tells you more than a guess does. You run it twice, on top of the ablation, the test topic and the length study's four teach-only runs of final, two of them 60-minute lessons through your pipeline. Spread what can be spread: the baseline's runs at the start, the length study's c1-skills runs once PREREG.md is committed, and final's runs as early as you can.

Some habits that keep the cost down, all through the project:

  • Check /usage in a Claude Code window before each big run.
  • Iterate on uv run p3 bench --config live --quick, and on --only with the one scenario you are changing: a round about the quiz needs --only quiz, not a teach scenario as well.
  • Try every new subagent, review loop or pipeline on an --only run before a full one.
  • Make full runs only of snapshots you mean to keep, and spread them over several days where you can.
If a usage limit stops a run

p3 bench stops at the first scenario that reports a usage limit, keeps the repeats before it and the scenarios of the stopped repeat that had finished, in bench/runs/_live/_aborted/, which git ignores, and prints the command to type. Wait for the reset (/usage shows when) and type uv run p3 bench --resume, which runs only the missing scenarios and saves the repeat as one run. uv run p3 check prints a WARN line while a stopped repeat is waiting. In the meantime, do the work that needs no Claude: your test topic, your reviews of runs you already have, the blind comparison once its runs exist, and the write-up. When it breaks has the details.
